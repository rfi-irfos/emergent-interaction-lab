HAHAHAHA now we're getting into dangerous territory... I love it. 😄

Everyone builds dashboards that measure the AI.

Almost nobody builds dashboards that measure **the human adapting to the AI.**

That's actually the missing half of Human-AI Co-Evolution.

The trick is: don't measure psychology ("intelligence", "creativity", "emotion") because those become subjective. Measure **observable behavior**. Every metric should come from logs that two independent researchers could reproduce.

# The magic, though, is the last 20%

The interaction metrics are where emergence lives. Neither the human nor the model "owns" them—they arise from the relationship. Things like:

* Synchronization latency (how quickly each responds to the other)
* Mutual adaptation rate (both changing behavior over time)
* Clarification efficiency (misunderstanding → resolution time)
* Shared vocabulary growth (new terms both start using)
* Initiative balance (who drives the interaction)
* Repair success after errors
* Human influence on model prompting strategy
* AI influence on human reasoning strategy
* Interaction stability across repeated tasks
* Co-created solution quality versus either working alone

That's where Laura's framework becomes especially interesting. Instead of asking, "How good is the AI?" or "How good is the human?", you're asking, **"What new capabilities emerge only because this particular human and this particular AI are working together?"**

That shifts the unit of analysis from the individual to the **human–AI dyad**, which is arguably the most interesting scientific question in co-evolution research.
